This policy explains what personal data EVENTPEDIA collects, how and why we use it, who receives it, how long we keep it, and what you can do about it. It is written to meet the Thai Personal Data Protection Act B.E. 2562 (PDPA) and Apple's App Store rules. This policy is also available in Thai. If the two versions differ, the English version applies.

The short version

  • You can browse EVENTPEDIA without giving us your name, email or phone number. The app creates an anonymous account for you automatically.
  • If you create a profile, your username, display name, bio and link are public. So are the hosts and venues you follow, and any event you publish.
  • Your saved events and your blocked list are private.
  • Your location is used only to find events near you, and only if you allow it. We do not store it.
  • We use PostHog to understand how the app is used. It never receives your search text, your exact location or your profile text.
  • We do not sell your data, use advertising networks, show ads targeted using your data, or track you across other companies' apps. Events that businesses pay us to promote are labelled.
  • Settings → Delete account removes your account and most of your data. Some things stay, such as events you published. Section 9 lists exactly what, and how to ask us to remove the rest.

2. What we collect and how

2.1 Your anonymous account (automatic)

When you first open the app, it creates an anonymous account on our server automatically, before you do anything. The account has a random account ID. It has no name, email address, phone number or password. The app keeps the key to this account in your iPhone's Keychain so you stay signed in. Our hosting provider's sign-in system also keeps technical records for the account, such as when it was created and last used, and the IP address and device type of its sign-in sessions.

The app creates a new anonymous account again after you delete your account.

2.2 Your profile (optional, public)

You only need a profile to save events, follow hosts and venues, report or block, and create events. A profile contains:

  • a username and a display name (required);
  • a short bio and a link (optional);
  • your app language (English or Thai). It starts as English and changes if you switch the language in Settings;
  • when the profile was created and last changed.

This information is public. The app does not currently show other people's profiles, but anyone, including people who do not use the app, can read it through our public data interface. Please do not put anything in your profile that you would not want to be public. If you publish an event, your display name is also shown as its host name (see 2.7). We do not ask for a profile photo.

2.3 Saved events (private)

When you save an event we store which event you saved and when. Only you can see your saved events.

2.4 Follows (public)

You can follow hosts and venues from an event's page. We store who you follow and when. Follows are public: anyone can read which account ID follows which host or venue through our public data interface, and the app shows follower counts. When a host you follow publishes an event, you get a notification in the app (see 2.9).

2.5 Blocks (private)

If you block someone we store who you blocked and when. Only you can see your blocked list (Settings → Blocked accounts). We do not tell the other person, but they may notice that they can no longer see your profile. The Terms explain exactly what blocking does.

2.6 Reports

You can report an event from the bottom of its page. You choose one reason from a fixed list (spam or fake event, wrong or misleading details, offensive or harmful, impersonating someone, something else). There is no free-text box. We store the reason, the event, the time and your account ID.

The host is not told who reported their event. Our moderators' review screen shows how many reports an event has and the reasons, not who filed them. Only people with administrative access to our database can see the reporter's account ID. We keep it so we can handle reports properly, for example to count one report per person. If you delete your account, your account ID is removed from your reports and the rest of each report is kept.

2.7 Events you create

When you create an event, the app saves it to our server as a draft as soon as it has a title, and again shortly after each change. An event contains what you enter: title, description, category, venue, date and times, price, number of spots, tags, whether it is a community event, and an optional cover image. It is linked to your account ID.

  • Drafts are visible only to you. A cover image you add to a draft is the exception: it is a public file (see Cover images below).
  • Published events are public. Everyone can see them in the app, and anyone with a shared link can see them on our event web page without the app. Your display name is shown as the host name. Changes you make after publishing appear straight away. The link between a published event and your account ID can be read through our public data interface.
  • When you publish, people who follow you as a host get a notification in the app with your host name and the event title.
  • You can cancel a published event. It then disappears from public view, but it is not deleted and you can publish it again. The app does not let you delete events or drafts yourself. Email us and we will delete them.

Cover images. You pick a cover image from your photo library. Before upload the app resizes it (1280 pixels wide) and saves it as a new JPEG file without the original photo's metadata, such as where and when it was taken or the camera model. The image is uploaded as soon as you choose it, even for a draft. It is stored as a public file that anyone can view, even while the event is a draft or after it is cancelled, and the file's address contains your account ID. The image stays online if you cancel the event or delete your account, until we delete it at your request.

2.8 Venues you add

If you add a new venue while creating an event, we store its name, area and address, linked to your account ID. Venues are part of a shared, public list that every host can choose from, and the link to your account ID can be read through our public data interface.

2.9 Notifications in the app

When a host you follow publishes an event, our server adds a notification to the list behind the bell icon in the app. It contains the host name, the event title and links to them. Only you can see your notifications. We store whether you have read them.

2.10 Location (only with your permission)

The app asks for your location only when you tap Current location in the event filters, and only while you are using the app. It never uses your location in the background. If you say no, everything stays centred on Bangkok and keeps working.

If you allow it, your device's position is sent to our server:

  • when you use Current location, and
  • when you open the map later. The map then centres on your position and looks for events around it.

We use the position only to find events near you. We do not save it in our database, we do not keep a location history, and we never send it to our analytics provider. Analytics only records whether you chose Bangkok, your current location or a point on the map, not where you are. When you move the map or use Choose on map, the app sends that point instead of your position. The blue dot showing your position on the map is drawn by iOS on your device. You can turn location off at any time in iOS Settings → Eventpedia.

2.11 Photos (only the ones you pick)

The app asks for access to your photo library only when you tap to add a cover image to an event. Only the photo you pick is uploaded, processed as described in 2.7. The app does not use your camera. You can change photo access at any time in iOS Settings → Eventpedia.

2.12 Searches

When you search for events or venues, the text you type is sent to our server to run the search. We do not save searches in our database. Because the search text is part of the request address, it may appear in our hosting provider's short-lived technical logs (see 2.14). It is never sent to analytics: analytics records only how long the search was and how many results it found.

2.13 Analytics (PostHog)

We use PostHog, hosted in the European Union, to understand how the app is used so we can fix problems and improve it. The app sends PostHog:

  • events about how you use the app, such as opening it, screens viewed, events viewed, saved or shared (with the event's ID), filters and categories chosen, searches (length and number of results only), whether you followed or blocked (not who), creating, publishing or cancelling an event, which profile fields you changed (not what you wrote), and views and taps of sponsored placements and of our own advertising banner;
  • an identifier: before you have a profile, a random ID created by the app; once you create a profile, your account ID. PostHog then joins your earlier activity to your account ID. The app also sends a random ID for the app installation;
  • device information: device model (for example "iPhone 16"), iOS version, app version and build, language, time zone and screen size;
  • your IP address, because the app connects to PostHog directly. PostHog may use it to work out an approximate location, such as your country or city.

PostHog never receives your search text, your exact location, your username, display name, bio or link, or the text of events you create. The app does not record your screen, does not send crash reports, and does not send an event when you report something or tap a ticket link.

We use analytics to count how often sponsored placements and our own advertising banner are seen and tapped, so we can measure and price them. We do not use analytics to target ads, and we do not give advertisers data about individual users.

The app has no in-app switch to turn analytics off. You can object to analytics, or ask us to delete your analytics data, by emailing [FILL: CONTACT EMAIL] (see section 11).

2.14 Technical logs

When the app connects to our hosting provider (Supabase) or to PostHog, their systems record technical details of each request, such as your IP address, the device or app type (user agent), the time and the address of the request. Our event web page creates the same kind of logs for anyone who opens a shared link. Supabase keeps these logs for no more than 7 days, for security and to keep the service running. At PostHog, the same technical details arrive with the analytics data and are kept with it: our PostHog plan lets us use that data for 1 year (free plan) or 7 years (paid plans), and it may stay stored at PostHog after that period (see 2.13 and section 8).

2.15 Emails you send us

Contact us in Settings opens a draft in your own mail app. The draft includes the app version and build, your iOS version and the app language, and you can edit or delete anything before sending. If you send it, or any other email to us, we receive your email address, the name on your email account and whatever you write.

2.16 Data on your iPhone, links and sharing

The app stores some things on your device: the key to your account (in the Keychain), your language choice (also saved to your profile if you have one, see 2.2), and whether a one-time promotion has already been shown. PostHog's software keeps its IDs and any analytics events waiting to be sent in the app's storage. Temporary files for share images, calendar files and cover uploads are kept in the app's cache.

Opening links: Get Tickets, View source, map directions and profile links open in your browser or in the relevant app, and that website's or app's own privacy policy applies. When you share an event, the link contains the event and the language, not who you are. Add to calendar creates a calendar file on your device; the app never reads your calendar. Some event images may load directly from the organiser's or source website, which then sees your IP address but not your account.

2.17 Event listings from public sources

We gather listings from public sources such as event calendars, venue and organiser websites and their public social media pages; our team also compiles listings by hand, and some come from partners. A listing can include the organiser's or host's name and the venue's details, and we keep a copy of the source data and a link to it. Our team reviews each of these listings before it is published, and published listings are public. Organisers and others named in a listing can ask us to correct or remove it by emailing [FILL: CONTACT EMAIL].

3. What we do not collect

  • No sign-in with email or phone number, and no passwords.
  • No contacts, calendar, camera, microphone or clipboard contents.
  • No profile photos.
  • No push notification tokens. The app does not ask for notification permission.
  • No advertising identifier (IDFA). The app does not ask to track you, and we do not track you across other companies' apps or websites.
  • No private messages: the app has no chat or comments.
  • No payment details: there are no purchases in the app.

We do not sell your personal data, and we do not share it with advertisers or data brokers.

5. Do you have to give us this data?

No law requires you to give us personal data. The anonymous account and the technical data in 2.13 and 2.14 come with using the app at all. You need a profile to save events, follow hosts and venues, report events, block someone and create events; without one, those features are not available. Location and photo access are optional: without location the app centres on Bangkok, and you can publish an event without a cover image.

9. Deleting your account

Open Settings (the gear icon on the Profile tab; if the Create profile screen opens, tap Not now first) and tap Delete account. If you have a profile, you type your username to confirm. Deletion happens straight away and cannot be undone. The app then starts a new, empty anonymous account.

What is deleted:

  • your profile: username, display name, bio, link and language;
  • your saved events;
  • everything you follow, and any follows of your profile;
  • your blocked list, and any blocks of you;
  • your in-app notifications;
  • your anonymous account and its active sign-in sessions. If the empty sign-in record cannot be removed automatically, the app tells you. Nothing else is attached to it any more, and you can email us to have it removed.

What stays after deletion:

  • Events you published stay public, with your display name as the host name. They are no longer linked to your account.
  • Your host name stays on your host record, and people who followed you as a host keep following it.
  • Cover images you uploaded stay online as public files. Their web address still contains your former account ID.
  • Drafts and cancelled events stay in our database. No one can see them.
  • Venues you added stay in the shared venue list.
  • Reports you filed stay, without your account ID.
  • Notifications already in other people's lists that show your host name and event title.
  • Your analytics history at PostHog, linked to your former account ID.
  • The random installation ID that PostHog's software created when you first opened the app. It stays in the app's storage and is still sent to PostHog until you uninstall the app, so PostHog can connect your new anonymous account with your earlier analytics history.
  • Emails you sent us; entries about your account in the audit log of our hosting provider's sign-in system, which records IP addresses (see section 8); and technical logs until they expire.

If you want any of this removed, email [FILL: CONTACT EMAIL]. Tell us your username and the events concerned, ideally before you delete your account, so we can find them. We will reply within 30 days.

10. Uninstalling the app does not delete your data

Deleting the app from your iPhone does not delete your account or anything stored on our servers. iOS may also keep the account key in the Keychain, so reinstalling the app may bring back the same account. To delete your data, use Settings → Delete account before you uninstall.

Because we do not ask for an email address or phone number, we may not be able to identify your account if you lose your device. If that happens, email us with your username and any details you remember, and we will try to help.

11. Your rights

Under the PDPA you have the right to:

  • access your personal data and get a copy of it, and ask how we obtained data you did not give us;
  • receive your data in a commonly used electronic format and have it sent to someone else (portability);
  • object to our use of your data, including for analytics;
  • ask us to delete your data or make it anonymous;
  • ask us to restrict how we use your data;
  • ask us to correct data that is wrong or incomplete;
  • withdraw your consent at any time where we rely on it, without affecting what we did before;
  • complain to the Office of the Personal Data Protection Committee (PDPC), www.pdpc.or.th.

You can do some of this yourself in the app: edit your profile in Settings → Edit profile, remove saves and follows, and delete your account in Settings → Delete account. You can turn off location or photo access at any time in iOS Settings → Eventpedia.

For anything else, email [FILL: CONTACT EMAIL]. We may ask for details that show the account is yours, such as your username. We will respond within 30 days, free of charge. If the law allows us to refuse a request, we will tell you why.

The app has no switch to turn analytics off. If you object to analytics, we will delete the analytics data linked to your account ID and tell you how we will handle future analytics data about you.

12. Children

EVENTPEDIA is not for anyone under 18, and the Terms require users to be 18 or older. The app does not ask for your age. If we learn that we hold data about someone under 18, we will delete it. If you think a child is using the app, please contact us.

13. Security

The app connects to our servers and to PostHog only over encrypted connections (HTTPS). The key to your account is stored in the iPhone Keychain. Our database rules limit who can read what: for example, only you can read your saved events, blocked list and notifications, and no user can read individual reports, including the person who filed them. Our moderation tools are available only to approved team members. No system is completely secure, but if a data breach is likely to affect your rights, we will notify the PDPC and, where required, you, as the PDPA requires.
