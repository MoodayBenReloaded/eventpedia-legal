Accounts

On first launch the app creates an anonymous account so you can browse and save events immediately. It is tied to your device, stored in the device keychain, and contains no personal information.

If you create a profile we store the username and display name you choose, and optionally a short bio, a link, a profile picture, and your language preference. Adding an email address is optional; its only purpose is to let you recover your account on another device. We verify it with a one-time code. We do not send marketing email.

Location

Location is optional. The app asks only when you open the map view. If you decline, the map simply centres on Bangkok and everything else keeps working.

If you allow it, your coordinates are sent to our backend to run the distance query and are not written to our database. We do not keep a location history, and we do not track you in the background — the app requests foreground location only.

Analytics

We use PostHog (EU-hosted, eu.i.posthog.com) to measure product usage: screens opened, events viewed and saved, searches, filters, shares, and notification permission outcomes.

Analytics are identified by a random id only. We never send your email address, username, or display name to PostHog. Before you create a profile, events carry a device-generated anonymous id; afterwards they carry your account id. Signing out returns you to a fresh anonymous id.

Analytics are switched off entirely in our development builds, so our own testing never enters the data.

Push notifications

If you enable notifications we store a push token for your device so we can deliver them. Tokens are sent to Expo's push service, which passes them to Apple or Google for delivery. You can turn notifications off per type in Settings, or entirely in your device settings; when a token stops working we mark it inactive and stop sending to it.

Where your data is stored

Our database, authentication and file storage run on Supabase in the Singapore region. Analytics are processed by PostHog in the European Union. Push delivery is handled by Expo and by Apple's and Google's notification services.

Who can see what

Your profile (username, display name, bio, link, picture) and any events you publish are public. Your saved and going events, follows, blocked accounts, notification settings and push tokens are private to you — enforced in the database, not just in the app.

Blocking is mutual in effect: a blocked account cannot see you and you cannot see them, in either direction.

Deleting your account

Settings → Delete account. You will be asked to type your username to confirm. This permanently deletes your profile, saved and going events, follows, blocked accounts, notifications, notification settings and push tokens. It cannot be undone.

One thing survives, deliberately. Events you published stay in the public catalogue under your host name, and are unlinked from your account. This is so people holding tickets to your event are not stranded by your account deletion. If you want a published event removed as well, cancel it before deleting your account, or contact us at [FILL: CONTACT EMAIL].

Event listings from other sources

Some listings are gathered from public event calendars and venue websites. For those we publish facts only — name, venue, date and time, category and price — together with a link to the original listing. We do not copy descriptions or artwork from other sites. If you are a source or a rights-holder and want a listing changed or removed, contact [FILL: CONTACT EMAIL] and we will act on it.
